## 11. Key Conclusions

1. **Burnout prevalence of 42%** (work-related) is 2.5x higher than the 16.6% reported by Al Subhi et al. (2020) in OMSB residents, suggesting a significant worsening trend.

2. **R2 is the most vulnerable training year** — highest depression (PHQ-9 9.1), highest anxiety (GAD-7 8.6), and lowest wellbeing (WHO-5 48.7%). Targeted support interventions should prioritize this cohort.

3. **Anesthesia residents are the most affected specialty** — work burnout 66.1, PHQ-9 10.2, both the highest of any program.

4. **Clear on-call dose-response** — work burnout increases linearly with call frequency (32 to 49), providing evidence for policy review of on-call schedules.

5. **Major life events are the strongest burnout risk factor** — +14.7 points personal burnout, the largest effect size observed.

6. **Biophysical signals are emerging** — moderate+ depression shows 18% lower HRV and 8% higher resting HR, supporting the hypothesis of altered autonomic tone in burnout. Longitudinal within-person analysis with more blocks will strengthen these findings.

7. **The ICU paradox** — ICU rotations show the lowest burnout despite high acuity, suggesting that structured teamwork and clear protocols may be protective.

8. **Significant improvement from baseline to Block 11** — work burnout decreased 6.8 points (t=-3.04, p<0.01), and patient burnout decreased 8.6 points (t=-3.10, p<0.01). This may reflect seasonal effects (summer block) or a Hawthorne effect from study participation.
