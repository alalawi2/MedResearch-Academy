What the pilot includes

Built for practice, with room to improve.

The prototype includes guided exercises, recordings, text responses, targeted retries and a phrase notebook. Native build 5 adds bundled AI-generated partner voices and optional OpenAI transcription after explicit consent for invited voice-beta testers. Build 9 adds an optional adaptive AI partner that replies to confirmed words with generated speech, within the six fictional case situations. It requires internet, beta access and separate consent. An optional web voice pilot lets you speak with an AI simulated partner; microphone audio is sent to OpenAI after you choose Start and grant permission. Live captions can be wrong and are temporary.

Live voice is still being tested. Native mobile live voice, subscription payments and validated speaking assessment are not ready. The scenarios are awaiting independent clinical and language-educator review. Wardwise is not a clinical assessment, exam score predictor or treatment tool. Use fictional details only.

Before you join

Who is Wardwise for?

Nurses who want a place to rehearse workplace conversations in English, particularly when moving into an English-speaking team. The initial focus is communication at work rather than exam preparation.

Can I practise without speaking aloud?

Yes. Guided practice supports typed responses and a targeted retry, so you can rehearse in a quiet or shared space.

What will it cost?

Wardwise is free during the beta. There are no subscriptions or in-app purchases in this release. Optional cloud transcription and adaptive AI practice require a free pilot access code and have usage limits. Any future paid offer will be announced separately; the beta does not enrol you in a paid plan.

Will it grade my pronunciation or clinical ability?

No. The current prototype does not provide pronunciation scores, clinical competence assessments or predicted exam results. AI conversation is practice, and its responses can contain errors.

Can I enter a real patient’s details?

No. Use only the fictional case facts supplied in the exercise. Do not enter names, dates, recordings or other information about real patients.
