# The Association Between Healthcare Workers' Burnout and Biophysical Parameters
## Interim Study Report — July 2026

**MREC #3190 | SQU-EC297-2023 | Royal Hospital EC Approved**
PI: Dr. Mohamed Al Rawahi | Co-PI: Dr. Abdullah M. Al Alawi
Sites: SQUH, Royal Hospital, Armed Forces Hospital — Muscat, Oman

---

## 1. Study Overview

A multi-center prospective pilot cohort study using the Copenhagen Burnout Inventory (CBI), validated psychological instruments (PHQ-9, GAD-7, ISI, WHO-5), and WHOOP wearable biosensors to quantify the relationship between burnout and objective biophysical markers in OMSB residents. Data collection began June 2026 across 13 four-week rotation blocks.

**Enrollment:** 72 participants (71 active, 1 withdrawn). 100% WHOOP connectivity. 99% baseline completion. 16 residency programs, 3 hospital sites. ~5,000 total data points collected.

## 4. Contributing Factors

### 4.1 Specialty

| Program | n | Burnout | Work CBI | HRV (ms) | RHR (bpm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Anesthesia** | 3 | **100%** | 73.8 | 46.1 | 68.9 |
| **ENT** | 5 | **100%** | 52.2 | **39.3** | **71.8** |
| **Emergency Medicine** | 6 | **83%** | 50.6 | **36.5** | 69.3 |
| Psychiatry | 4 | 75% | 36.6 | 42.7 | 74.6 |
| General Surgery | 12 | 67% | 52.4 | 45.0 | 66.8 |
| Internal Medicine | 23 | 52% | 50.3 | 61.5 | 64.9 |
| Pediatrics | 5 | 40% | 40.0 | 63.0 | 61.3 |
| Orthopedics | 3 | 33% | 34.5 | 72.5 | 60.7 |

ENT and Emergency Medicine show the lowest HRV in the cohort (39.3 and 36.5 ms), indicating objective autonomic dysregulation consistent with chronic stress.

### 4.2 Call Patterns (Block 11 Follow-up, N = 58)

| Call Type | n | Burnout | Work CBI | PHQ-9 | WHOOP Sleep | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **24-hour on-call** | 25 | **60%** | 43.3 | 7.1 | **6.0h** | **57.2%** |
| 12-hour shift | 18 | 39% | 40.7 | 4.3 | 6.3h | 60.7% |
| Home call | 2 | 50% | 41.0 | 13.5 | 6.9h | 62.2% |
| Other/Mixed | 13 | 46% | 33.5 | 7.2 | 6.9h | 63.0% |

24-hour on-call residents have 60% burnout, the shortest objective sleep (6.0h), and lowest recovery scores. 12-hour shift residents show significantly lower burnout (39%) and better recovery.

### 4.3 Weekly Work Hours

| Hours/week | n | Burnout | Work CBI |
|---|---|---|---|
| <40 hours | 16 | **25%** | 26.8 |
| 40-60 hours | 31 | 58% | 44.5 |
| 60-80 hours | 11 | **64%** | 47.7 |

Clear dose-response relationship: burnout more than doubles from <40h (25%) to 60-80h (64%).

### 4.4 Marital and Parental Status

| Factor | n | Burnout | Work CBI | PHQ-9 | HRV (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Married | 32 | **66%** | 48.1 | 5.8 | **45.6** |
| Single | 36 | 61% | 45.5 | 8.1 | 56.6 |
| Has children | 16 | 62% | 48.4 | 5.4 | 51.2 |
| No children | 54 | 61% | 45.5 | 7.3 | 53.3 |

Married residents show higher burnout (66% vs 61%) and significantly lower HRV (45.6 vs 56.6 ms), suggesting work-family conflict as a physiological stressor. Paradoxically, single residents report higher depression (PHQ-9 8.1 vs 5.8), suggesting different distress pathways.

### 4.5 Gender

| Gender | n | Burnout | Work CBI | PHQ-9 | GAD-7 | HRV | RHR | WHOOP Sleep |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Male | 26 | **69%** | 44.4 | 6.2 | 5.2 | 55.4 | **61.8** | 5.8h |
| Female | 44 | 57% | 47.2 | 7.2 | 7.0 | 51.3 | 69.0 | **6.6h** |

Males have higher burnout prevalence (69% vs 57%) but females report higher depression and anxiety scores. Females sleep nearly 1 hour more per night objectively.

### 4.6 Exercise

| Exercise | n | Burnout | Work CBI | HRV (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 days/week | 24 | 62% | 49.0 | **47.7** |
| 1-2 days/week | 29 | 66% | 46.1 | 53.7 |
| 3-4 days/week | 16 | **56%** | 42.9 | **59.0** |

Residents exercising 3-4 days/week show lower burnout (56%) and 24% higher HRV (59.0 vs 47.7 ms) compared to sedentary peers.

### 4.7 Caffeine

| Caffeine/day | n | Burnout | Work CBI | PHQ-9 | WHO-5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 9 | **33%** | 41.3 | 7.1 | 52% |
| 1-2 | 48 | 65% | 46.8 | 6.2 | 52% |
| 3+ | 13 | **69%** | 47.3 | 9.1 | **44%** |

High caffeine intake (3+/day) is associated with 69% burnout, highest depression (PHQ-9 9.1), and lowest wellbeing (WHO-5 44%).

### 4.8 Psychiatric History

### 5.2 Burnout Severity Gradient (Work CBI Quartiles)

| Quartile | n | CBI Range | HRV (ms) | RHR (bpm) | Recovery | Strain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (lowest burnout) | 17 | 7-32 | **58.6** | **64.9** | 59.1% | 7.1 |
| Q2 | 17 | 32-46 | 55.7 | 66.6 | 58.3% | 7.4 |
| Q3 | 17 | 46-57 | 48.9 | 66.7 | 59.2% | 6.9 |
| Q4 (highest burnout) | 19 | 61-93 | **48.5** | **67.0** | 61.5% | 7.4 |

A consistent HRV gradient across burnout quartiles: **17% lower HRV** from Q1 to Q4, with progressive elevation in resting heart rate. This supports burnout as a measurable autonomic phenomenon.

### 5.3 Self-Reported vs Objective Sleep (N = 70 paired)

| Group | Self-Reported | WHOOP-Measured | Discrepancy |
|---|---|---|---|
| All residents | 6.2h | 6.3h | -0.1h (accurate) |
| Burnout YES (n=43) | 6.1h | **6.4h** | -0.3h (under-report) |
| Burnout NO (n=27) | 6.4h | 6.2h | +0.2h (over-report) |

Burned-out residents perceive their sleep as worse than objective measurement shows (-0.3h discrepancy), suggesting altered sleep perception as a burnout feature.

## 8. Key Conclusions

1. **Burnout is endemic** — 61% prevalence at baseline, consistent with international literature (35-67% in residents).

2. **HRV is the strongest biophysical correlate** — burned-out residents show 25% lower HRV (46.7 vs 62.5 ms), with a consistent dose-response gradient across burnout quartiles. This is the study's most significant finding, supporting burnout as measurable autonomic dysfunction.

3. **24-hour on-call is the highest-risk work pattern** — 60% burnout, shortest sleep (6.0h), lowest recovery (57.2%). 12-hour shifts show 39% burnout — a 21 percentage-point difference favoring shorter shifts.

4. **Work hours show a clear dose-response** — burnout more than doubles from <40h/week (25%) to 60-80h/week (64%).

5. **Specialty matters** — ENT (100%), Anesthesia (100%), and Emergency Medicine (83%) carry the highest burden. Their HRV values (36-46 ms) indicate chronic sympathetic overdrive.

6. **Marriage increases burnout risk** — married residents show 66% burnout with significantly lower HRV (45.6 vs 56.6 ms), suggesting work-family conflict manifests physiologically.

7. **Exercise is protective** — residents exercising 3-4 days/week show lower burnout (56% vs 62%) and 24% higher HRV. Sedentary residents were over-represented among those who worsened over time.

8. **Major life events nearly double burnout** — 73% vs 42% during the affected block.

9. **Sleep perception is altered in burnout** — burned-out residents perceive worse sleep than WHOOP measures, suggesting subjective distortion as a burnout feature.

10. **Early longitudinal signal is encouraging** — burnout prevalence decreased from 64% to 50% at first follow-up, with 52% of residents showing meaningful improvement.
